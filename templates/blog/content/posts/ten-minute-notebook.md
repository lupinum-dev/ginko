In September I gave myself one rule: ten minutes at the same bench by the river, every morning, notebook open. No camera, no app. Just what I could see and hear from there.

## The format

I wrote the same five lines each day, so the pages could be compared:

```text
Date, time, weather
Where I stood
What I saw (count, don't guess)
What I heard
One question to look up at home
```

The last line matters most. It turns a walk into homework you actually want to do.

## What the page noticed

By the third week, the notes showed patterns I had walked past for years. The grey herons stand on the weir only when the water is low. The wagtails come to the gravel bank after rain, not before. And the loudest bird on most mornings was a wren I never once saw.

> 7:40, light rain. Two grey herons on the weir, one with a fish. Wren singing in the ivy, again invisible.

That is the whole entry for day 19. It is not exciting. But read thirty of them in a row and the river starts to look like a place with a timetable.

## Try it

Pick a spot you pass anyway. Ten minutes is short enough to keep and long enough for the birds to forget you. Write before you look anything up.
