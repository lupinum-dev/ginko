This winter I started a list of every tree on my street, one block each Sunday. The street has four blocks, so the list was done by February. It has 61 trees on it, in five kinds.

## Learning trees without leaves

Most guides start with leaves. In March there are none, so I learned the trees by their bark, their buds and their shape instead. It is easier than it sounds:

- **Plane:** bark that flakes off in patches, leaving a map of grey, green and cream. Round seed balls still hang from the branches.
- **Lime:** twigs that zigzag from bud to bud, with small, shiny red buds.
- **Norway maple:** buds in pairs, opposite each other on the twig.
- **Horse chestnut:** big, sticky brown buds. You will not mix it up with anything else.
- **Rowan:** a small tree with dark, woolly buds; the birds took its berries in autumn.

## What the list is good for

Now I see the street differently. The planes are the oldest trees and stand where the old houses are; the young rowans were planted where the road was rebuilt. Two gaps in the row are stumps, cut last year. I have written to the city to ask what will be planted there.

Next month the leaves come out, and I get to check every line of the list.
