In November we hung a seed feeder on the bike shed in our back yard and put a shallow dish of water under the hedge. Then I watched for ten minutes each morning before work.

## Who came first

The great tits found the feeder on the second day. The blue tits came a day later and took one seed at a time, flying off with each to eat it in the hedge. The house sparrows took a week to trust it, but once they did, they came as a group of eight and stayed.

The robin never used the feeder. It waited below and picked up what the others dropped, and it chased every other robin out of the yard.

## The water mattered more than we thought

On frosty mornings, more birds came to the water dish than to the seeds. A blackbird bathed in it at minus two degrees. We now break the ice before breakfast and refill it with lukewarm water.

## Keeping it clean

Seed left damp goes mouldy, and a dirty feeder can spread disease between birds. Every two weeks we wash the feeder with hot water, let it dry and refill it. We clear the husks below it at the same time.
