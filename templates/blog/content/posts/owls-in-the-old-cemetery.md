The old cemetery closes its gates at dusk in winter, but the path along its wall stays open. On still January evenings, that path is the best place in the city to hear tawny owls.

## Two voices

The long, wavering “hoo-hoo-hoooo” is a male. The sharp “ke-wick” is usually a female answering. In the weeks around the turn of the year, pairs call to each other a lot as they settle their territories for spring.

## How to listen

Go on a night without wind, an hour after sunset. Walk slowly along the wall, stop often and stay quiet for a few minutes each time. Leave the torch off: your eyes get used to the dark, and the owls are not disturbed.

Do not play recorded calls to make them answer. To an owl, a recording is an intruder in its territory, and it wastes energy it needs in the cold.

## What we heard

On our last visit we heard at least two pairs, one near the chapel and one by the yew trees at the far end. We never saw them. That is normal, and it is enough.
