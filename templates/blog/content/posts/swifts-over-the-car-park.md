Around nine on a warm evening, the sky above the supermarket car park fills with swifts. They race low between the lamp posts in tight groups, screaming, then climb out of sight and come back again.

## Where they live

Not in the car park. The swifts nest in the old flats across the road, in gaps under the roof tiles that most people never notice. Swifts need such gaps in buildings, and they come back to the same one year after year. When a roof is renewed and the gaps are closed, the birds lose their home.

## Birds of the air

A swift does almost everything in flight. It catches insects, drinks from the river in a low pass and, most nights outside the breeding season, sleeps on the wing. On the ground it is nearly helpless: its legs are tiny, and a swift that falls into the open struggles to take off.

That is why the car park suits them so well. It is open, warm in the evening and full of insects rising from the hot tarmac.

## Gone by August

The swifts arrived in the first week of May. By the first week of August they will be gone, on their way to Africa, while the swallows stay for weeks more. So if you want to hear them, go out on the next warm evening. Look up from the trolley return.
