Most people ride the tram to its last stop by mistake. I ride it on purpose, on the greyest mornings, because the forest behind the turning loop is at its best in fog.

## The route

From the stop, cross the road and take the wide path past the allotments. After ten minutes the gardens end and the beeches begin. Keep left at the first fork; the path climbs gently for about two kilometres to a clearing with a bench and a table. From there, the loop back to the tram is another three kilometres, mostly downhill.

All of it is on firm paths. Good shoes are enough, and you are back at the stop in under two hours.

## What to look for

Beeches keep their dead leaves low down well into winter, so the lower branches glow copper while the crowns are bare. Look at the trunks too: the smooth grey bark shows old carved initials, stretched wide as the tree has grown around them.

In fog, the forest gets quiet in a way the city never does. You hear the drip from the leaves, a nuthatch somewhere above, and the tram bell from very far away.

## Before you go

The tram runs every twenty minutes on weekends, less often early in the morning. The clearing has no shelter, so bring a layer for the bench.
