Under a roof, the air changes quickly: warm and dry in the afternoon, cool and damp at night. Lime plaster takes up moisture and gives it back slowly, so the rooms feel calmer, and it does not grow mould easily.

It also takes light in a way paint does not. The surface is never quite even, so the low sun from the high windows draws soft shadows across the wall in the evening.

Lime asks for a little care. It marks more easily than paint, and a mark is best rubbed out with a dry sponge, not washed. Small cracks in the first year are normal; the plasterer comes back once to close them.
